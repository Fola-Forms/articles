The United States temporarily suspended visa processing worldwide, affecting green card applicants already facing lengthy backlogs with more uncertainty about their application status. The Department of State paused all visa processing to train embassy and consular staff on new public charge rules, which could deny applications if an individual appears likely to rely on government benefits.

## What changed

The State Department pause is largely affecting applicants using Consular Processing because it targets immigrant visa interviews at overseas diplomatic posts. This pathway is commonly used by spouses of U.S. citizens, certain employment-based applicants and some refugees and asylum recipients who become eligible for permanent residence. After an immigrant petition is approved, the case moves through the National Visa Center before an interview is scheduled at a U.S. embassy or consulate; if approved, the applicant receives an immigrant visa, travels to the United States and then becomes a lawful permanent resident.

This action arrives after a series of Trump administration measures that have slowed or paused different parts of the legal immigration system, including green card processing for some refugees, asylum seekers and nationals of countries targeted by travel restrictions.

## Why it matters

The interview suspension directly stalls the final step before applicants can travel to the U.S. and obtain permanent resident status. Consular applicants—particularly spouses of U.S. citizens and employment-based visa holders—are now in queue suspension with no stated timeline for interviews to resume. The stated reason is staff training on public charge rules, meaning adjudicators will apply stricter scrutiny to whether applicants are likely to become a "public charge" and will therefore make benefits-assessment decisions more uniformly across posts.

Practitioners advising clients abroad should expect:
- Extended wait times before interview scheduling
- More rigorous public charge review once interviews resume
- Possible requests for additional evidence about financial support, employment prospects, and family ties

## Way forward

- **Confirm your client's status**: If your client has an approved immigrant petition and is pending a consular interview, contact the relevant embassy or consulate for current interview scheduling information and any new documentation requirements.
- **Prepare for enhanced public charge scrutiny**: Assemble evidence of financial stability (bank statements, employment letters, affidavits of support) and review your client's full financial profile before the interview resumes.
- **Monitor State Department updates**: Check the DOS website and the National Visa Center portal regularly for announcements on when interviews will resume.
- **Inform affected clients**: If you're handling cases in Consular Processing, notify clients of the pause and set realistic expectations for timelines.
