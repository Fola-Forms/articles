A U.S. District Court for the Central District of California issued a preliminary injunction blocking the U.S. government from conducting warrantless civil immigration arrests in the district unless the arrestee is deemed likely to escape before a warrant can be procured. The ruling, issued by U.S. District Judge Maame Ewusi-Mensah Frimpong, also bars the government from restricting attorney access at the downtown Los Angeles ICE detention facility.

## What changed

The court order requires that "Defendants are enjoined from enforcing their policy and practice of making warrantless civil immigration arrests in this District without a prearrest individualized determination by the arresting agent or officer of probable cause that the person being arrested is likely to escape before a warrant can be obtained, as required by 8 U.S.C. 1357(a)(2) and 8 C.F.R. § 287.8(c)(2)," as signed by Judge Frimpong.

The judge's order notes that "Mere presence within the United States in violation of United States immigration law is not, by itself, sufficient to conclude that a person is likely to escape before a warrant for arrest can be obtained." The injunction applies to Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, Ventura, Santa Barbara and San Luis Obispo counties.

## Why it matters

The court found that the plaintiffs had "established a substantial likelihood of an unlawful policy and practice by defendants of conducting warrantless civil immigration arrests without probable cause." This ruling creates enforceable legal standards within the Central District of California that differ materially from prior practice.

For practitioners with clients in the covered counties, the injunction means:

- **Arrest challenges**: You can now argue that ICE arrests violating the flight-risk requirement are unconstitutional under the Fourth Amendment and potentially subject to suppression or civil damages claims.
- **Detention access**: Attorneys have a court-backed right to immediate access to detained clients at the downtown LA ICE holding facility, addressing prior complaints of legal access denial.
- **Documentation burden**: ICE agents must now document specific facts supporting their flight-risk determination before making a warrantless arrest—creating a paper trail that can be scrutinized post-arrest.

The ruling is persuasive authority in other circuits and may influence litigation strategy in nearby districts.

## Way forward

- **If you represent a client arrested without warrant in the Central District**: Challenge the arrest by filing a motion to suppress, citing the injunction and demanding ICE produce the pre-arrest flight-risk determination.
- **If a detained client is in the LA holding facility**: Assert your right to immediate attorney access under the injunction; report any access denials to the court immediately.
- **For preventive advice**: Counsel clients in the covered counties to document their ties to community (employment, family, housing, etc.) to rebut any future flight-risk claim if enforcement contact occurs.
- **Monitor compliance**: The ruling may be appealed or modified; track any appellate filings and court status updates through PACER or local immigration bar networks.
