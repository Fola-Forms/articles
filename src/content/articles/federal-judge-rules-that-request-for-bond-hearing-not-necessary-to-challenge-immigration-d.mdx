A Colorado federal judge has ruled that people detained by immigration authorities do not have to exhaust a request for a bond hearing before an immigration judge before they can challenge their detention directly in federal court. The decision clarifies the path to federal habeas relief and affects how you advise detained clients on remedies.

## What changed

A federal judge in Colorado ruled last week that it is not mandatory for a person to seek a bond hearing before an immigration judge before they challenge the lawfulness of their detention in federal court. This ruling removes what some immigration authorities have asserted as a procedural prerequisite to federal habeas corpus relief. Previously, practitioners and detainees faced uncertainty about whether immigration authorities could deny a habeas petition on the ground that the petitioner had not first requested bond relief in immigration court.

## Why it matters

The ruling directly impacts your client intake and filing strategy. You no longer face pressure to first pursue an administrative bond hearing request if the evidence suggests your client has strong habeas grounds. This accelerates access to federal court review, which has proven more receptive to detention challenges than immigration courts in many jurisdictions, including Colorado.

The Denver-based federal appeals court ruled on Tuesday against the government's broad assertion of authority to keep people in immigration custody without the opportunity to demonstrate their suitability for release on bond. The most common allegation is that the government is improperly denying bond hearings to people who are eligible by law. Colorado's judges, like the vast majority of their peers nationwide, have almost uniformly agreed that the government is improperly detaining people without the opportunity for release. The federal judge's clarification of the habeas pathway means your client can proceed directly to the more favorable forum.

This is especially significant given the procedural obstacles and delays in immigration court bond hearings. You can now advise detained clients with confidence that federal court habeas relief is available as a direct remedy without exhausting immigration court procedures first.

## Way forward

- **For detained clients**: If your client is in immigration custody and you believe detention is unlawful, you can now file a federal habeas corpus petition without delay. Do not let government authorities argue that a bond hearing request is a prerequisite.

- **Draft and file promptly**: Gather evidence of the government's failure to provide a lawful bond hearing or of other detention law violations and prepare your habeas petition for filing in the appropriate federal district court.

- **Preserve the record**: Document any denials of bond hearings, delays in scheduling hearings, or substantively defective hearings that immigration judges have conducted, as these strengthen your federal court case.

- **Check local circuit law**: Confirm the legal standard in your circuit—Colorado is in the 10th Circuit, which has issued favorable precedent on this issue, but confirm applicability to your specific factual scenario.
