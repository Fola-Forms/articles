DHS has published an interim final rule authorizing USCIS to mandate electronic filing for immigration forms that have been available online for at least 180 days. The rule takes effect August 11, 2026, and shifts the immigration system away from paper filings and physical lockbox payments toward a fully electronic submission process.

## What changed

USCIS may now require any immigration form available for e-filing for a minimum of 180 days to be submitted electronically going forward. Before mandating e-filing for a form, USCIS must publish advance notice on its website at least 60 days in advance.

When a form becomes mandatory e-filing, applicants and petitioners must file through an online account—either by completing the form directly in the online interface or by uploading a PDF of the signed form along with supporting evidence. The rule eliminates the use of the U.S. Department of Treasury's physical lockbox services and accelerates the transition to electronic fee payment.

USCIS will grant waivers from the e-filing requirement on a case-by-case basis for undue hardship, at the agency's discretion.

The interim final rule is now effective and open for public comment until October 13, 2026. The full text is available in the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-16313/mandatory-electronic-filing-e-filing).

## Why it matters

This rule systematizes what has been a gradual shift: electronic filing is already available for many USCIS forms, but this rule gives the agency authority to *require* it form-by-form without waiting for a new Federal Register notice each time. Once a form has been e-filings-ready for six months, USCIS can flip the switch with two months' notice.

**Timeline impact:** The 60-day advance notice gives you time to retool client intake procedures, update checklist systems, and prepare for mandatory online filing. If you represent applicants with limited digital literacy or hardware access, plan now to submit waiver requests before e-filing becomes mandatory for high-volume forms (I-485, I-130, I-140, I-765, etc.).

**Operational changes:** No more printing, mailing to a lockbox, or waiting for USCIS receipt notices tied to mailroom date-stamps. E-filing compresses the receipt timeline and should reduce errors from illegible handwriting or missing pages. However, it also requires reliable internet access and a myUSCIS account for each filer—something your clients must have in place before the deadline.

**Security and efficiency:** USCIS frames this as fraud-detection and identity-vetting leverage: electronic data is easier to cross-reference, search, and analyze than paper. The agency also cites faster adjudication and reduced incomplete submissions as benefits.

## Way forward

- **Audit your current workflow.** Identify which forms you file most often and check the [USCIS e-filing page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539-online) to see which are already e-ready. Plan to migrate to online filing for those forms now, before they become mandatory.

- **Watch for the 60-day notices.** USCIS will post mandatory e-filing notices on its website. Set a reminder to check the newsroom monthly, or subscribe to USCIS alerts, so you catch the deadline before your clients are locked out of paper filing.

- **Prepare waiver language.** Draft template waiver requests for clients without reliable internet, computer access, or English literacy. USCIS discretion means documentation of hardship matters.

- **Update client intake.** Confirm that all clients can create and access a myUSCIS account before filing. Test the online filing interface yourself so you can troubleshoot tech issues and guide clients through upload, signature, and payment steps.
