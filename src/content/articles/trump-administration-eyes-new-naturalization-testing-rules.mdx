The Trump administration is moving to overhaul how the U.S. naturalization civics test is administered and scored. The Trump administration is considering changes to the U.S. citizenship test that could establish new educational standards for naturalization applicants and give immigration officials greater flexibility in how the exam is administered, according to a federal regulatory agenda.

## What changed

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) said the rule would establish "a framework and a standard" for applicants to meet citizenship education requirements, as the department seeks to "further enhance the integrity of the naturalization test." 

Two major elements are under consideration:

- **New educational standards.** DHS will establish a formal framework defining what civics and English-language education requirements applicants must demonstrate.
- **Third-party testing administration.** USCIS is also considering allowing third parties to administer the naturalization test, a change it said would provide additional flexibility in how the exams are conducted, per the DHS proposal.

The agenda entry does not provide further details about how such a system would operate. DHS is expected to publish a notice of proposed rulemaking in December.

## Why it matters

The proposal could affect hundreds of thousands of citizenship applicants each year. For immigration practitioners, this announcement signals that significant changes to N-400 naturalization adjudication are on the horizon—and you need to prepare clients and plan filing strategy accordingly.

The introduction of third-party testing administration could reshape how USCIS conducts interviews and assessments at field offices. New educational standards may raise or lower the bar for eligibility or require applicants to document language proficiency and civics knowledge in ways they do not today.

Until the NPRM is published and the specific standards are detailed, you cannot advise clients precisely on how the test will change. But you should begin tracking this rule closely: The agency has not released details on how the educational standards or testing procedures could change and said it is still considering the proposal's potential costs and benefits.

## Way forward

- **Monitor the December 2026 Federal Register.** When DHS publishes the NPRM, download and analyze it at [regulations.gov](https://www.regulations.gov). Mark the public comment deadline on your calendar.
- **Track how third-party testing could work.** Consider whether partnerships with CBP, language schools, or community organizations might administer civics exams, and what quality and fairness safeguards would apply.
- **Advise clients on filing timing.** Counsel clients approaching N-400 eligibility on the risks and benefits of filing before versus after the new rule takes effect. Earlier filings may be subject to current (less stringent) standards.
- **Prepare for a formal rulemaking.** Participate in the public comment period if the final rule affects your practice. Cite case law, client data, or operational feasibility concerns.
