USCIS biometrics — fingerprints, photograph, and signature collected at an [Application Support Center (ASC)](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/find-a-uscis-office) — feed the [FBI Name Check](https://www.fbi.gov/services/cjis/name-checks) and interagency security vetting that gate most benefit adjudications. The regulatory authority sits at [**8 CFR §103.16**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.16) and [**8 CFR §103.17**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.17), with operational guidance in [**USCIS Policy Manual Volume 1, Part C, Chapter 2**](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-1-part-c-chapter-2). USCIS's [March 9, 2023 update to the Policy Manual on biometrics reuse](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-1-part-c-chapter-2) consolidated the reuse rules that had grown piecemeal across [COVID-era](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/uscis-response-to-covid-19) policy memos and the [2021 H-4/L-2/E biometrics-waiver settlement](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/legal-docs/Edakunni_PSA.pdf).

## What changed

**Statutory and regulatory backbone.** [**8 CFR §103.17(a)**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.17) provides that USCIS may require any applicant or petitioner to submit biometrics, and that the biometrics fee — currently **$30** for most forms under the [2024 fee rule (89 FR 6194)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/31/2024-01331/u-s-citizenship-and-immigration-services-fee-schedule-and-changes-to-certain-other-immigration), effective April 1, 2024 — is collected at filing or as a separate notice. For some forms ([N-400](https://www.uscis.gov/n-400), [I-485](https://www.uscis.gov/i-485)), the biometrics fee is bundled into the form fee; for others ([I-539](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539), historically), it was separately listed.

**The 2023 reuse policy.** Under [USCIS PM Vol 1 Part C Chapter 2](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-1-part-c-chapter-2), USCIS may **reuse** previously-captured biometrics — fingerprints, photo, and signature — for new or pending requests when the prior capture is on file, the applicant has previously been fingerprinted by USCIS for an immigration benefit, and the underlying [FBI Name Check](https://www.fbi.gov/services/cjis/name-checks) and security checks remain valid. Reuse eliminates the need for a new ASC appointment in most renewals and follow-on petitions. The applicant still receives an [I-797C](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/uscis-account-update-form-i-797c) notice but it confirms reuse rather than scheduling an appointment.

**The 2021 H-4/L-2/E biometrics waiver.** Under the [Edakunni v. Mayorkas settlement](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/legal-docs/Edakunni_PSA.pdf), USCIS suspended the biometrics requirement for most [I-539](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539) applicants in [H-4](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-4-eligible-dependent-spouses-and-children), [L-2](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/l-2-spouse-or-children-of-l-1-employee), and [E](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/e-3-certain-specialty-occupation-professionals-from-australia) dependent categories — initially through May 2023, then extended. The waiver substantially eliminated the ASC bottleneck that had previously delayed dependent extensions for months and decoupled them from the principal's [I-129](https://www.uscis.gov/i-129) timeline.

## Why it matters

Biometrics are the bottleneck most likely to put a benefit case past published processing times — and the most likely to be misunderstood by clients.

**Reuse is the default for most renewals; not all.** Under [USCIS PM Vol 1 Part C Chapter 2](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-1-part-c-chapter-2), reuse applies when (a) prior biometrics are on file with USCIS, (b) the applicant remains the same person on the same A-Number, and (c) no derogatory information has been added that requires a new check. An applicant whose prior biometrics were captured by [CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/) at port-of-entry, by [Department of State](https://travel.state.gov/) at a consular interview, or by an ASC for a [SENTRI/Global Entry](https://www.cbp.gov/travel/trusted-traveler-programs/global-entry) trusted-traveler program does **not** qualify for USCIS reuse — those captures sit in different systems.

**Triggers for a new ASC visit:**

1. **First-time benefit applicant** with no prior USCIS biometrics on file.
2. **Age change crossing the 14-year threshold.** USCIS does not collect biometrics from applicants under 14; reaching 14 during pendency triggers a new capture under [8 CFR §103.16](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.16).
3. **New A-Number** assigned (rare — happens when an A-Number was misassigned and later consolidated).
4. **New criminal record** since the last biometrics, requiring an updated [FBI Name Check](https://www.fbi.gov/services/cjis/name-checks).
5. **Court order** in connection with a [removal proceeding](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/find-immigration-court-and-access-internet-based-hearings) or a [criminal sentencing](https://www.uscourts.gov/) requiring updated biometric capture.
6. **Quality failure** on the prior capture — smudged fingerprints, missing image — flagged by the FBI on return.
7. **Specific benefit categories** that statutorily require fresh biometrics even with reuse on file — [N-400](https://www.uscis.gov/n-400) naturalization is the leading example because the [good-moral-character](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-12-part-f) inquiry runs current.

**Missing the appointment is a denial trigger.** [8 CFR §103.2(b)(13)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.2#p-103.2(b)(13)) and the [I-797C appointment notice](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/uscis-account-update-form-i-797c) make clear that failing to appear without a timely rescheduling request is treated as abandonment of the underlying benefit request. USCIS issues a denial on abandonment grounds, leaving the petitioner to file a new petition (and pay a new fee) to restart.

**Rescheduling is allowed but bounded.** The [USCIS Contact Center](https://www.uscis.gov/contactcenter) (1-800-375-5283) or the [online reschedule portal](https://my.uscis.gov/) accepts requests "for good cause" — illness, work emergency, weather, travel. The request must arrive before the original appointment date when possible. USCIS issues a new I-797C with a revised date, typically within 30–60 days.

## Way forward

- **Check the [I-797C](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/uscis-account-update-form-i-797c) on receipt.** It says "Biometrics Appointment Notice" or "Biometrics Reuse Notice." A reuse notice requires no action; the applicant simply waits for adjudication. An appointment notice has a date, time, and ASC address — show up.
- **Bring required documents.** The appointment notice itself plus government-issued photo ID ([passport](https://travel.state.gov/), [state driver's license](https://www.dhs.gov/real-id), [I-94](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/), [I-551 green card](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card)) for I-9-like verification at ASC intake.
- **Reschedule by [my.uscis.gov](https://my.uscis.gov/) or the [Contact Center](https://www.uscis.gov/contactcenter) before the appointment date.** A no-show without a request is harder to recover from than a documented reschedule.
- **For [N-400](https://www.uscis.gov/n-400) and [I-485](https://www.uscis.gov/i-485) cases, expect biometrics within 30–60 days of receipt.** Delays past that timeline are a flag — call the Contact Center and submit a [case inquiry through the e-Request portal](https://egov.uscis.gov/e-request/).
- **For [I-539](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539) dependent renewals, monitor the [Edakunni biometrics waiver](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/legal-docs/Edakunni_PSA.pdf) extension status** — the waiver has been periodically renewed and could revert. Plan accordingly for in-progress extensions.
- **For employer-sponsored applicants traveling internationally, do not depart between biometrics scheduling and the appointment.** USCIS treats failure to appear as abandonment regardless of cause, and re-entering the U.S. for a domestic biometrics visit on a tight timeline is risky.
