A federal district court in Massachusetts has postponed the effective date of the Department of Homeland Security's final rule replacing "duration of status" (D/S) with fixed admission periods for affected F, J, and I nonimmigrants. The rule had been scheduled to take effect on September 15, 2026. This development gives practitioners, schools, exchange programs, and employers breathing room to reassess transition plans while litigation proceeds.

## What changed

On September 14, District Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV issued a memorandum and order in Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration v. United States Department of Homeland Security, postponing the rule's implementation. The immediate development is a postponement of implementation—not a final judgment invalidating the rule.

The regulation, titled "Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission and an Extension of Stay Procedure for Nonimmigrant Academic Students, Exchange Visitors, and Representatives of Foreign Information Media," was published in the Federal Register on July 17, 2026, and was scheduled to take effect Sept. 15. Under the rule (had it taken effect), F-1 students, J-1 exchange visitors and certain I-1 foreign media representatives, who have long been admitted for the duration of their authorized educational program, exchange program or qualifying employment, would instead receive fixed periods of admission.

The court has scheduled an in-person status conference for October 2, 2026, at noon Eastern Time, in Courtroom 1. That conference date should not be treated as the postponement's expiration date. The docket notice schedules a hearing; it does not establish when the rule will take effect.

## Why it matters

The postponement gives you time to plan without an immediate cliff. Students, exchange visitors, institutions, and employers should update September 15 transition planning in light of the court's action and confirm applicable agency instructions before taking individual filing or travel steps.

However, do not assume the rule is dead. The postponement is temporary—the litigation is ongoing, and DHS may appeal or seek to lift the stay. The October 2 status conference is only a procedural check-in, not an expiration date for the postponement.

A critical trap: Employers must continue evaluating permission to remain and permission to work separately. The postponement is not itself an EAD extension. For example, continued employment during a pending STEM OPT extension depends on the separate requirements for that extension, including timely filing and the applicable 180-day limit. The court order postpones the rule's effective date; it does not extend work authorization or OPT on its own.

## Way forward

- **Confirm applicable rules now.** Institutions should preserve transition preparations while confirming which steps remain necessary under the operative rules and subsequent agency guidance. Do not abandon compliance work; instead, verify which parts of your September 15 plan still apply under current SEVIS, ELIS, and work-authorization rules.

- **Monitor the docket.** The October 2 status conference and any further court orders will determine next steps. Subscribe to updates from the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts (Case No. 26-cv-[case number]) or your legal counsel.

- **Advise clients separately on work and stay authorization.** If a student or exchange visitor has a pending OPT extension, STEM extension, or other work-authorization filing, confirm those deadlines and requirements independently. The D/S postponement does not grant extra time for those applications.

- **Prepare contingency plans for both scenarios.** The rule may ultimately take effect, be struck down, or face further delay. Work with counsel to develop advice that covers F-1 students nearing the end of their programs, J-1 exchange visitors on long-term programs, and employers relying on OPT timelines.
