A federal appellate judge from the Denver-based 10th Circuit has asked the U.S. Department of Justice to investigate whether immigration authorities are withholding bond hearings for detainees months after a binding court ruling required them. The request, made on the record during oral argument, signals potential enforcement pressure and may affect detention strategy for practitioners in Colorado and five neighboring states.

## What changed

Colorado's judges largely agreed that detainees deserve bond hearings, and the 10th Circuit handed down a binding interpretation confirming that view in June. On September 15, a three-judge panel heard the government's appeal in another detention case.

At the end of oral arguments, Judge Harris L. Hartz asked whether the Justice Department is "accepting our precedent as controlling" in the 10th Circuit's six-state region and whether the Department of Homeland Security is complying. Hartz then stated, "Let me tell you what I heard last Tuesday because it was disturbing to me. And you may want to look into this. In fact, I'd ask you to look into this and report back to this court within a week."

Judge Hartz, a George W. Bush appointee from New Mexico, explained that he attended a legal event and was surprised that the 10th Circuit's recent decision rejecting the government's view on bond hearings, aligning with nearly all other decisions from multistate circuit courts, had not stopped the flood of habeas challenges to detention without bond—hundreds of cases still.

## Why it matters

In every one of those hundreds of habeas cases, ultimately the district court orders a bond hearing, but DHS is not doing it otherwise. This pattern suggests that immigration detainees are waiting months for bond hearings despite judicial orders—prolonging unnecessary detention and increasing habeas filings.

For 10th Circuit practitioners, this development means:

- **DHS non-compliance may be systemic.** If the DOJ confirms a pattern, the circuit may impose sanctions, expedited review, or other remedies that shift burden or timeline.
- **Habeas petitions remain the primary tool.** Until DHS complies with the June ruling across the board, detainees will continue filing federal habeas corpus petitions to enforce their right to hearings.
- **Judge's formal request creates accountability.** A federal judge asking for a one-week report elevates the issue and makes non-compliance a matter of record for future appellate decisions or sanctions motions.

## Way forward

If you represent a client detained in the 10th Circuit region:

- **File a habeas petition promptly.** The precedent is your client's right, but DHS may not honor it without a court order. Do not assume DHS will comply with the June ruling on its own.
- **Flag delays in writing.** Document any month-long waits for bond hearings and reference the pattern Judge Hartz identified. This becomes evidence of bad faith if you later seek sanctions or expedited relief.
- **Watch for the DOJ's response.** The DOJ is due to report back to the court within a week. That filing will be public and may reveal the scope of non-compliance and any remedial steps the government commits to.
- **Consider transfer challenges.** If DHS threatens out-of-state transfer after your client is entitled to a hearing, cite the 10th Circuit's related ruling blocking transfers to preserve jurisdictional continuity.
