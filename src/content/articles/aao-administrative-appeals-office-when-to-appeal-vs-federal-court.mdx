The [USCIS Administrative Appeals Office (AAO)](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/organization/directorates-and-program-offices/administrative-appeals-office-aao) reviews a defined slate of denial decisions on a de novo standard. The catalogue is at [**8 CFR §103.1(f)(3)(iii)**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.1) and the procedure at [**8 CFR §103.3**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.3). Knowing whether the AAO has jurisdiction — and whether AAO is faster than going straight to federal court — is half the strategy of a post-denial challenge. The AAO publishes its [**Practice Manual**](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/legal-docs/AAO_Practice_Manual.pdf) and a [precedent-decisions index](https://www.uscis.gov/administrative-appeals/aao-decisions/precedent-decisions); both are the starting point for any AAO filing.

## What changed

The AAO's current jurisdictional list, last comprehensively restated when [USCIS published the consolidated Practice Manual on December 20, 2017](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/legal-docs/AAO_Practice_Manual.pdf), covers most employment-based petitions and a long tail of humanitarian and waiver categories. The notable exclusions matter as much as the inclusions:

**AAO has jurisdiction over** (selected list — confirm against the [current Practice Manual](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/legal-docs/AAO_Practice_Manual.pdf)):

- [Form I-140 immigrant worker](https://www.uscis.gov/i-140) denials — EB-1A, EB-1B, EB-1C, EB-2 (including [NIW](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-f-chapter-5)), EB-3.
- [Form I-129](https://www.uscis.gov/i-129) denials for [H-1B](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations-and-fashion-models), [L-1](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/l-1a-intracompany-transferee-executive-or-manager), [O-1](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement), [P-1](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/p-1a-internationally-recognized-athlete), [R-1](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/r-1-temporary-religious-workers), [TN](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/tn-nafta-professionals), [E-3](https://www.uscis.gov/e-3-certain-specialty-occupation-professionals-from-australia), and others.
- [Form I-360](https://www.uscis.gov/i-360) denials — VAWA, special immigrant religious workers, [SIJS](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/permanent-workers/employment-based-immigration-fourth-preference-eb-4/special-immigrant-juveniles), Amerasian.
- [Form I-526 / I-526E](https://www.uscis.gov/i-526) EB-5 denials.
- [Form I-601 / I-601A](https://www.uscis.gov/i-601a) waiver denials.
- [Form N-600](https://www.uscis.gov/n-600) certificate of citizenship denials.
- Most [revocations under 8 CFR §205.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-205.2) of the petitions above.

**AAO does NOT have jurisdiction over** — and this is where mistakes happen:

- [Form I-130](https://www.uscis.gov/i-130) family-based petition denials. These go to the [Board of Immigration Appeals (BIA)](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/board-of-immigration-appeals) under [8 CFR §1003.1(b)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-V/section-1003.1#p-1003.1(b)(5)), with a separate [EOIR Form EOIR-29](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/page/file/1083026/download) appeal form and a $110 fee.
- [Form I-485](https://www.uscis.gov/i-485) adjustment-of-status denials. No administrative appeal exists. Review is by [motion to reopen/reconsider under 8 CFR §103.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.5), by renewal of the application before an [Immigration Judge](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/find-immigration-court-and-access-internet-based-hearings) in removal proceedings under [8 CFR §1245.2(a)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-V/part-1245/section-1245.2), or by [APA challenge in federal district court](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/706).
- [Naturalization N-400](https://www.uscis.gov/n-400) denials. These go to a [USCIS hearing under N-336](https://www.uscis.gov/n-336) and then to [district court under INA §310(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1421&num=0&edition=prelim).

## Why it matters

The AAO's review is de novo on the administrative record — meaning the panel considers the case fresh, on the existing file, without deference to the field-office or service-center decision. That is a real advantage on a legal-error denial. But the AAO is not fast. [Recent AAO processing times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/) for I-140 appeals run roughly 6–12 months; complex EB-5 appeals can run longer. If the petitioner needs a benefit in 90 days — a worker about to fall out of status, a beneficiary about to age out under [CSPA](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-eligibility/child-status-protection-act-cspa) — the AAO is the wrong forum.

Two situations point straight to federal court:

**Unreasonable delay.** USCIS sitting on a benefit request well past published processing times is reviewable as agency action "unlawfully withheld" under [5 U.S.C. §706(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/706) and as a [mandamus claim under 28 U.S.C. §1361](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1361). The [TRAC factors](https://casetext.com/case/telecommunications-research-action-cen-v-fcc), drawn from [Telecommunications Research & Action Ctr. v. FCC, 750 F.2d 70 (D.C. Cir. 1984)](https://casetext.com/case/telecommunications-research-action-cen-v-fcc), govern. There is no AAO equivalent for delay.

**Legal error on a category outside AAO jurisdiction.** A denied [I-485](https://www.uscis.gov/i-485) on a non-discretionary ground — say, a USCIS misreading of the [212(a)(9)(B) unlawful-presence bar](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-9-part-b) — is reviewable in district court under [5 U.S.C. §706(2)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/706) for legal error. The [§1252(a)(2)(B)(i) jurisdictional bar](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1252&num=0&edition=prelim) on review of discretionary denials, as construed in [Patel v. Garland, 596 U.S. 328 (2022)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/20-979_h3cj.pdf), narrows but does not eliminate the path.

## Way forward

- **Confirm jurisdiction before drafting.** Open the [AAO Practice Manual](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/legal-docs/AAO_Practice_Manual.pdf) Chapter 3 (jurisdiction) and verify the denied form is on the list. Filing an [I-290B](https://www.uscis.gov/i-290b) on a [BIA-jurisdiction I-130](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/board-of-immigration-appeals) wastes the $675 filing fee and the 30-day window.
- **Calendar 30 days from the date of the decision.** [8 CFR §103.3(a)(2)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.3#p-103.3(a)(2)(i)) sets a 30-day window; revocations get 15 days under [§103.3(a)(2)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.3#p-103.3(a)(2)(i)). Add three mailing days under [8 CFR §103.8(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.8).
- **File the I-290B with a brief — or reserve the right to file a brief later.** The form lets the petitioner submit the appeal and supplement with a brief within 30 days. Reserving is fine if the merits are complex; missing the 30-day appeal filing is fatal.
- **Cite [AAO precedent decisions](https://www.uscis.gov/administrative-appeals/aao-decisions/precedent-decisions).** Decisions like [Matter of Dhanasar, 26 I&N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016)](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/page/file/920996/download), [Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010)](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/07/25/3567.pdf), and [Matter of Skirball Cultural Center, 25 I&N Dec. 799 (AAO 2012)](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/07/25/3736.pdf) are binding on the field. Cite them when the field-office officer applied a stricter rule than precedent allows.
- **Pair the appeal with parallel relief when timing is tight.** A worker out of status during a 9-month appeal needs a [bridge filing strategy](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual) — premium [I-129](https://www.uscis.gov/i-129) with a new employer, [B-2 change of status](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539), or a [§245(k)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1255&num=0&edition=prelim) backstop on the next AOS.
- **Pull the trigger on federal court when AAO timing won't work.** Pre-filing demand letter to the field-office director, then complaint in the appropriate [U.S. District Court](https://www.uscourts.gov/) — venue under [28 U.S.C. §1391(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1391) is wherever the plaintiff resides or where the defendant agency office sits.
