U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services played a key investigative role in a federal case that resulted in Ana Zahia Gonzalez, 46, being found guilty of naturalization fraud. This conviction signals USCIS's commitment to protecting the integrity of the naturalization process and demonstrates the agency's coordination with law enforcement to identify and prosecute immigration fraud schemes.

## What changed

A federal jury convicted Ana Zahia Gonzalez of naturalization fraud following an investigation in which USCIS collaborated with law enforcement partners. The case underscores the agency's active role in identifying applicants who obtain citizenship through fraudulent means, including false statements, concealed criminal histories, or misrepresented eligibility.

## Why it matters

Naturalization fraud undermines the integrity of U.S. citizenship and damages public trust in the immigration system. When applicants misrepresent facts on naturalization applications (Form N-400), they may gain citizenship unlawfully while excluding deserving applicants. USCIS investigations and prosecutions send a clear message that fraud will not be tolerated and can result in criminal conviction, imprisonment, and potential denaturalization. If you are aware of naturalization fraud or other immigration-related crimes, reporting it protects the system for legitimate applicants.

## Way forward

- **Review your N-400 carefully**: If you are applying for naturalization, ensure every statement on your Form N-400 is truthful and complete. Any false answers—even by omission—can constitute fraud and result in criminal prosecution.
- **Consult an immigration attorney**: Before filing your naturalization application, work with a qualified immigration lawyer who can review your history, identify any potential issues, and help you prepare accurate responses.
- **Report suspected fraud**: If you know of someone committing naturalization fraud or other immigration crimes, contact USCIS or call the tip line maintained by law enforcement partners.
- **Understand the consequences**: Conviction for naturalization fraud can result in criminal penalties, fines, imprisonment, and loss of citizenship status.
