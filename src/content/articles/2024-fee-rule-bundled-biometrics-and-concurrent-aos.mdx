USCIS published its final fee rule on January 31, 2024 (89 FR 6194),
effective April 1, 2024. It was the first comprehensive fee
restructuring since 2016, and it changed the per-form math for
almost every adjustment-of-status (AOS) package practitioners file.
This article walks through what changed, why it matters for
family-based and employment-based filers, and how to plan a clean
concurrent package under the new rule.

## What changed

Three structural shifts redefined the AOS fee math:

1. **Biometrics fold into the I-485.** The standalone $85
   biometric-services fee is gone for most categories. The cost of
   biometrics is now bundled into the I-485 base fee.

2. **Concurrent I-765 and I-131 drop to $0.** When a Form I-765
   (Application for Employment Authorization) or Form I-131
   (Application for Travel Document) is filed *concurrently with a
   pending or co-filed I-485*, USCIS charges no separate fee for
   those forms. Standalone filings — for example a renewal I-765
   for an existing AOS applicant — still carry the regular fee.

3. **Paper and online I-130s diverged.** A paper-filed I-130
   (Petition for Alien Relative) costs $675; the online filing is
   $625, a $50 incentive to file electronically. The same paper /
   online split applies to several other forms.

The full schedule is published in the
[Federal Register notice linked above](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/31/2024-01005/u-s-citizenship-and-immigration-services-fee-schedule-and-changes-to-certain-other-immigration);
USCIS also maintains a current
[fee calculator](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055) and the
[official fee schedule (G-1055)](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055)
that reflects later technical corrections.

## Why it matters

For a typical family-based concurrent AOS package — I-130 + I-485 +
I-765 + I-131 + I-864 — the new math is:

| Form  | Pre-2024 fee | Post-2024 fee (paper) | Notes |
| ----- | ------------ | --------------------- | ----- |
| I-130 | $535         | $675                  | $625 online |
| I-485 | $1,140 + $85 biometrics | $1,440 | biometrics bundled |
| I-765 | $410         | $0                    | concurrent with I-485 |
| I-131 | $575         | $0                    | concurrent with I-485 |
| I-864 | $0           | $0                    | unchanged |
| **Total** | **$2,745** | **$2,115** | savings only on concurrent packages |

The win is significant for clean concurrent filings — about a
**$630 reduction per applicant**. The penalty is for fragmented
filings: if the I-765 is filed AFTER the I-485 receipt, USCIS
treats it as a standalone request and the $520 paper / $470 online
fee applies. The same trap catches a late-added I-131 advance
parole.

For *employment-based* AOS, the same concurrent rule applies — an
I-485 with concurrent I-765 / I-131 incurs the bundled cost — but
the I-140 fee structure is separate ($715 paper) and never folds
into the I-485 calculation.

## Way forward

Three practitioner-side takeaways:

- **Build the package as one filing.** If your client's I-130 is
  already pending when the priority date becomes current, file the
  I-485 + I-765 + I-131 together in a single package, not
  trickling them in. The concurrent-discount rule rewards
  packaging.

- **Mind the renewal cliff.** The first I-765 renewal during a
  pending AOS reverts to the standalone fee
  ([$520 paper / $470 online](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055)). Set
  a reminder to file the renewal early so a card lapse doesn't
  cost the client work authorization while the renewal sits in
  the queue.

- **File the I-130 online when feasible.** The $50 saving on the
  I-130 paper-vs-online split is per applicant; on a 30-petition
  practice that's $1,500 a year of pure overhead. Online filing
  also produces a receipt within hours instead of weeks.
