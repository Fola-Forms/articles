Immigration courts in New York have adopted a new style of hearing meant to speed up deportations since the beginning of June. Respondents are receiving notice as little as a week prior for bulk appointments, with some hearings accelerated by multiple months without warning. This shift creates significant logistical and strategic challenges for immigration practitioners managing removal cases.

## What changed

New York immigration courts adopted a new hearing format starting in early June that consolidates large numbers of cases into single-day dockets. Judges nationwide now face immense single-day caseloads that can include well over a hundred individuals, a substantial departure from traditional individual hearing schedules. Notice periods have been compressed to as little as one week prior to bulk appointments, and some respondents have had their hearings accelerated by multiple months without advance warning.

The compressed timelines have affected respondents across a multi-state area. Individuals are required to attend hearings from places as far away as Las Vegas, New Jersey, and Chicago, often because address-change notifications have not been processed in the immigration court system.

## Why it matters

This practice directly impacts your case management and client communication strategy. Many respondents are not receiving notice in time and not showing up; failure to appear results in deportation orders. The rapid scheduling leaves little time for you to:

- Advise clients on case status and next steps
- Prepare witnesses or evidence
- Secure interpreters or expert testimony
- Coordinate travel for respondents from out-of-state locations
- File motions or continuances

The immense single-day caseloads place extraordinary strain on judicial dockets, potentially reducing the time judges spend on individual cases and increasing the risk of in absentia removal orders.

## Way forward

- **Confirm client address immediately.** Contact each client representing them in New York immigration court and verify their current address on file with the immigration court. Update addresses proactively to reduce the risk of missed notice.
- **Monitor court dockets weekly.** Check the immigration court's online docket system frequently for your cases, especially as hearing dates approach. Do not rely solely on USCIS or court notices arriving by mail.
- **Prepare for accelerated timelines.** Build in extra lead time when drafting pleadings or coordinating evidence. Expect that hearing dates may shift with little notice.
- **Document the impact.** If short notice materially harms your ability to prepare a defense or gather evidence, document the prejudice for potential appeal or remand arguments before the immigration judge.
