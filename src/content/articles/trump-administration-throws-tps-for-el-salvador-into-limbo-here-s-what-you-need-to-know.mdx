With TPS for El Salvador set to expire on September 9, 2026, the Trump administration took the unusual step of declining to either extend or terminate the status. The fate of roughly 170,000 Salvadorans who still have TPS remains in limbo while the administration prepares a formal announcement on the designation's future.

## What changed

On the day TPS was set to expire, USCIS posted a single message: "ALERT: An announcement on El Salvador's TPS will be made at the appropriate time. Until such announcement is made, Salvadoran individuals present in the U.S. under TPS retain protection including work authorization." No further explanation was provided.

The administration did not issue a formal extension of the designation, nor did it issue a termination notice with an 18-month delay period—the two standard outcomes under TPS law. Instead, it suspended the normal decision process, leaving the status of all El Salvador TPS beneficiaries in legal uncertainty.

For more than 25 years, Salvadorans have lived and worked legally in the United States, passing a background check every 18 months to renew their status. Despite the fact that they are now some of the most-vetted immigrants in the entire nation, most have no path to obtaining permanent status without Congress acting.

## Why it matters

This limbo creates immediate complications for practitioners and clients:

- **Work authorization continues for now.** USCIS explicitly confirms that current TPS beneficiaries retain protection and work authorization. Employers should treat existing Employment Authorization Documents (EADs) as valid until a formal termination or extension is announced.
- **No 18-month wind-down.** A termination with delay would have given beneficiaries 18 months to plan departure, seek alternative relief, or petition Congress. The current suspension offers no timeline and no certainty.
- **Re-registration is uncertain.** TPS designations normally require renewal every 18 months. It is unclear whether USCIS will accept re-registration filings for El Salvador TPS, or whether doing so constitutes a waiver of any future termination decision.
- **Precedent matters.** Since taking office, the Trump administration has terminated almost every single TPS designation which has come up for review, "delegalizing" more than 1 million people. The pattern suggests eventual termination is likely, even if delayed.

## Way forward

- **Do not assume this status is stable.** If you represent El Salvador TPS beneficiaries, treat this as a temporary reprieve, not a renewal or extension. Begin documenting how long they have been present and working, and whether they have U.S.-born children or other family ties that might create alternative immigration pathways.
- **Monitor USCIS announcements closely.** Check the USCIS website regularly for the promised formal announcement. Set a reminder to review the status in 30 days.
- **Explore alternative relief now.** For beneficiaries approaching 10 years of continuous presence, consider cancellation of removal (even if not currently deportable). For those with family sponsors, explore consular processing or spousal visas. For business owners, consider EB categories where applicable.
- **Advise clients on EAD renewal.** If an EAD is expiring soon, consult with USCIS on whether to attempt renewal or await the formal announcement. The USCIS website may offer updated guidance.
