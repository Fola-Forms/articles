USCIS's fraud detection efforts have resulted in guilty pleas from two individuals charged with conspiracy to commit visa fraud in connection with H-1B petitions. The case underscores the agency's ongoing vigilance against immigration fraud schemes and its cooperation with federal prosecutors.

## What changed

Sampath Rajidi, 51, and Sreedhar Mada, 51, have pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit visa fraud, as announced by U.S. Attorney Eric Grant. The guilty pleas represent a significant enforcement action stemming from USCIS's fraud investigation and detection work. The case demonstrates that individuals and schemes targeting the H-1B nonimmigrant worker visa program face serious federal criminal consequences, including charges under visa fraud statutes.

## Why it matters

H-1B fraud cases like this one send a clear signal to employers, immigration practitioners, and would-be fraudsters that USCIS actively identifies and refers cases involving false statements, misrepresentations, or conspiracy related to visa petitions. Guilty pleas reduce the likelihood of prolonged litigation and reflect the strength of USCIS's investigative findings.

For employers and practitioners, this enforcement action reinforces the importance of:

- Submitting truthful and accurate information on all H-1B petitions and supporting documents
- Ensuring that job descriptions, wages, and working conditions match what is claimed in the petition
- Maintaining proper documentation and file records
- Understanding that criminal penalties—including imprisonment—can attach to visa fraud conspiracies

## Way forward

If you are an employer or immigration professional working with H-1B cases:

- **Review your compliance protocols.** Audit your H-1B petition process to ensure all statements about job duties, compensation, and working conditions are accurate and verifiable.
- **Train your HR and recruiting teams.** Ensure staff understand that false statements on immigration petitions are federal crimes, not minor paperwork errors.
- **Consult legal counsel before filing.** Work with a qualified immigration attorney to review H-1B petitions for accuracy and completeness before submission.
- **Report suspected fraud.** If you become aware of H-1B fraud, you may report it to USCIS or the Department of Justice. Contact [https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/contact-us](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/contact-us) for guidance on reporting.
