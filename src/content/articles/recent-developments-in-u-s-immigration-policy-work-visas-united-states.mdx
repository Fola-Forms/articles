USCIS has overhauled its adjudication standards for the "good moral character" requirement in naturalization cases. The new guidance, issued in August 2025, shifts evaluation methodology and expands the factors officers must weigh—both in the applicant's favor and against them.

## What changed

USCIS issued a policy memo that significantly changes how immigration officers evaluate the "good moral character" requirement for naturalization applicants. The agency moved to a "totality of the circumstances" approach that goes beyond simply checking for disqualifying conduct.

Positive factors can include community involvement, family caregiving responsibilities, stable employment history, professional accomplishments, and educational achievements. Simultaneously, officers will now also review activities that include multiple DUI convictions, repeated traffic violations, harassment and any behavior that does not align with the "average behavior" of citizens in the community.

USCIS also issued new guidance on ideological screening. Officers will now consider whether foreign beneficiaries support "anti-American" ideologies or organizations when adjudicating various immigration benefit requests.

## Why it matters

The shift to "totality of the circumstances" is a double-edged sword. On one hand, applicants with prior issues now have a clearer pathway to argue rehabilitation and community contributions. On the other, the memo broadens what conduct officers *can* consider disqualifying—not just traditional statutory bars but also traffic violations and behavior deemed inconsistent with average community norms. This gives adjudicators more discretion and creates harder-to-predict outcomes.

The new ideological vetting component reaches beyond naturalization. Naturalization and other applicants may face heightened review not only of their moral character but also of their ideological affiliations and online activity. If you represent clients seeking any immigration benefit, you should anticipate social media review and prepare clients for questions about their affiliations and public statements.

## Way forward

- **For N-400 cases**: Begin by mapping positive equities early (employment, education, community service). Document stable residence and family ties. Prepare clients for questions about prior traffic violations, alcohol-related conduct, and online activity.
- **Client counseling**: Advise clients that their social media presence, public statements, and organizational affiliations may be reviewed. Discuss what posts or associations might trigger heightened scrutiny.
- **Evidence gathering**: Collect comprehensive evidence of rehabilitation (employment records, volunteer work, references, religious/civic participation) to offset any negative factors or address rehabilitation for past conduct.
- **Verify updates**: USCIS may issue updated N-400 instructions or policy guidance clarifying these changes. Check the USCIS website regularly for revised forms or memos.
