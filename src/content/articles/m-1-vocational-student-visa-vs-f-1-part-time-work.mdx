## What changed

USCIS maintains its M-1 vocational-student guidance in [Policy Manual, Volume 2, Part M](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-m), which sits alongside the F-1 academic-student guidance in Part F. The underlying regulation for M-1 students is at [8 CFR 214.2(m)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2#p-214.2(m)); consular procedure is covered in [9 FAM 402.5-6](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040205.html); and SEVP's operational page for vocational students is [Study in the States — M Students](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/prepare/eligibility-requirements-vocational-students).

The M-1 covers a much narrower population than the F-1. It applies to nonimmigrant students attending vocational or other nonacademic programs — culinary, cosmetology, flight training, technical trades, and similar — that grant a certificate, diploma, or non-academic credential rather than an academic degree. Students enrolled at community colleges in non-degree certificate programs may also land on M-1 depending on how the program is coded in SEVIS.

## Why it matters

Three structural differences from the F-1 catch students out — usually because they planned around the wrong status:

1. **Authorized stay is fixed, not Duration of Status.** Where an F-1 is admitted in D/S — for the entire course of study plus a 60-day grace period — an M-1 is admitted under [8 CFR 214.2(m)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2#p-214.2(m)(5)) for a fixed period equal to the program length plus 30 days, capped at one year total. Extension beyond that requires a [Form I-539](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539) Application to Extend/Change Nonimmigrant Status, and aggregate M-1 status (including extensions) cannot exceed three years.

2. **Employment is essentially prohibited.** M-1 students cannot work during the program of study under [8 CFR 214.2(m)(13)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2#p-214.2(m)(13)) — there is no on-campus work authorization analog to F-1, no CPT, and no off-campus work. The only employment-authorization path is post-completion practical training, capped at one month per four months of full-time study (six months maximum), authorized only by USCIS via [Form I-765](https://www.uscis.gov/i-765) on a [Form I-539](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539)-recommended basis with [Form I-538 Certification by Designated School Official](https://www.uscis.gov/i-538) and only after the program is complete.

3. **Change of status from M-1 to F-1 is statutorily barred where the program is the same.** Under [INA §248(a)(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1258&num=0&edition=prelim), an M-1 cannot change status to F-1 if the purpose is to qualify for a higher-degree program based on the M-1 training. The intent of this restriction is to prevent the M-1 from being used as a back door to F-1; a student who plans to ultimately pursue an academic degree should consider F-1 from the start.

The practical consequence is that the M-1 is the right tool for a discrete, time-bounded vocational credential — and the wrong tool for almost anything else.

## Way forward

A clean M-1 issuance follows the same backbone as F-1 but with tighter constraints. Each step matters because the M-1 has fewer escape valves than the F-1 when things go wrong.

**1. Confirm the school is SEVP-certified for the M category.** Many schools are certified for F-1 only; vocational programs that lead to a certificate require separate M certification. The status is checkable through [ICE's SEVP Certified School Search](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/school-search). A school certified F-1 only cannot issue an M-1 I-20.

**2. Receive an M-1 [Form I-20](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/i-20) from the Designated School Official.** The I-20 must explicitly indicate "M-1" in the visa class field and show the program start date, end date, total program length, and source-of-funds attestation. The DSO and the student both sign page 1.

**3. Pay the [I-901 SEVIS fee](https://www.fmjfee.com/i901fee/index.html) and file the [DS-160](https://ceac.state.gov/genniv/) for a consular interview.** The interview follows [9 FAM 402.5-6](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040205.html). Consular officers evaluate nonimmigrant intent under [INA §214(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1184&num=0&edition=prelim) — and M-1 applicants face particular scrutiny because a U.S. vocational credential often does not carry direct earning power in the home country, making "intent to return" harder to demonstrate.

**4. Observe the 30-day arrival rule.** As with F-1, an M-1 may not enter the United States more than 30 days before the program start date on the I-20, per [8 CFR 214.2(m)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2#p-214.2(m)(5)). The CBP officer admits the M-1 with a specific date-certain on the [I-94](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/) — not D/S — equal to program length plus 30 days, capped at one year.

**5. Enroll in a full course of study and report to the DSO.** SEVIS activation must occur within 30 days of the program start date, per [8 CFR 214.3(g)(3)(iii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.3#p-214.3(g)(3)(iii)). Full-time enrollment for M-1 is defined by the school's vocational-program standard — a clock-hour or credit-hour minimum the school specifies on its [Form I-17](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/i-17) petition for SEVP certification.

**6. Do not work — and decline any offers, paid or volunteer in your field.** There is no on-campus employment for M-1 students. Even structured volunteer work that displaces a paid position is unauthorized employment, per [USCIS Policy Manual Vol 2, Part M, Chapter 5](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-m). A single unauthorized-employment episode terminates the SEVIS record and creates removability under [INA §237(a)(1)(C)(i)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1227&num=0&edition=prelim).

**7. File for an extension if the program runs long.** An M-1 who needs more than the initial authorized stay must file [Form I-539](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539) before the I-94 expires, supported by an updated I-20. Total M-1 status — including extensions — cannot exceed three years per [8 CFR 214.2(m)(10)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2#p-214.2(m)(10)).

**8. Post-completion practical training is narrow and front-loaded.** If practical training is needed, the student files [Form I-765](https://www.uscis.gov/i-765) (category (c)(6)) together with [Form I-539](https://www.uscis.gov/i-539) and [Form I-538](https://www.uscis.gov/i-538) — but only after the program is completed. Authorized duration is one month per four months of full-time study, capped at six months. Training must directly relate to the program completed; no fishing expeditions.

**9. Plan the exit early.** Where an M-1 wants to continue with an academic program, the INA §248(a)(3) bar on M-1 → F-1 change of status (where the F-1 program is itself based on the M-1 training) often forces a consular return: depart, obtain an F-1 visa abroad, re-enter. Plan this six months in advance, not three weeks.
