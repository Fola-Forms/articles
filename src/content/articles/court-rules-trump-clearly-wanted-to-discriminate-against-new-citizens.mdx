A federal court blocked a USCIS policy from August 2025 that prevented nonpartisan groups from helping new citizens register to vote at citizenship ceremonies. U.S. District Judge Adam B. Abelson found that the ban was motivated by discriminatory intent. The ruling affects how voter registration assistance is provided at naturalization oath ceremonies.

## What changed

The USCIS policy prohibited nonpartisan groups from assisting new citizens with voter registration at citizenship ceremonies, restricting registration help to state and local election officials only. The League of Women Voters and National Council of Jewish Women sued the government over this restriction.

Judge Abelson ruled that the ban was motivated by discriminatory intent, citing government officials' statements—including USCIS Director Joseph Edlow's statement that immigrants threaten to reshape the nation electorally by becoming citizens. The court temporarily blocked (paused) the policy, meaning it cannot be enforced pending further proceedings.

## Why it matters

For immigration attorneys conducting citizenship ceremonies, this ruling clarifies what voter registration assistance is permissible at oath ceremonies. Nonpartisan third-party groups like the League of Women Voters and National Council of Jewish Women may now resume offering registration help at ceremonies, rather than restricting the service to election officials alone.

The court's finding of discriminatory intent—based on official statements about electoral impact—creates a strong precedent if the Trump administration attempts to defend or reinstate the policy on appeal. It also signals judicial scrutiny of policies framed in neutral administrative language but grounded in animus toward immigrants' political participation.

## Way forward

- **Confirm current ceremony protocols** with your USCIS field office to understand which voter registration assistance (if any) is currently being provided or permitted at your local oath ceremonies.

- **Notify clients** that nonpartisan voter registration groups may be available to assist them at citizenship ceremonies; do not assume such assistance is unavailable.

- **Document any restrictions** that remain in place at your local ceremony venue—this ruling does not automatically delete the policy nationwide, only pauses its enforcement. The case will continue in federal court.

- **Monitor the appeal** (if the government appeals Abelson's order) by checking [PACER](https://pacer.uscourts.gov) or asking your local EOIR / USCIS contact for updates on the case status.
