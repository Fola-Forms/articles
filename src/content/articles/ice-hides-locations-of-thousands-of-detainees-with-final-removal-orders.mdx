ICE has stopped publicly revealing where it is holding thousands of detainees who are subject to final deportation orders, making it far harder for attorneys and family members to locate them. ICE removed those detainees from its online detainee locator system on Sept. 15, and the American Immigration Lawyers Association warned its members about the situation Friday. This unannounced policy shift creates urgent challenges for anyone representing clients with final removal orders.

## What changed

ICE has stopped publicly revealing where it is holding thousands of detainees who are subject to final deportation orders, an unannounced move that has made it far more difficult for lawyers and relatives to find them. ICE removed those detainees from its online detainee locator system on Sept. 15, according to three current and former ICE officials who spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss the change.

The online detainee locator system, which was created in 2010 to allow relatives, attorneys and members of the public to determine whether someone is being held by ICE and where they are located, now excludes everyone with a final removal order. The new exclusion applies specifically to detainees with final removal orders.

**Limited exceptions:** The practice does not apply to detainees held at a detention facility in Adelanto, California or at temporary holding facilities in Minneapolis, New York City or suburban Chicago, because judges have ordered ICE to list detainees in those facilities in the online locator promptly upon their arrival, as part of litigation over their detention practices.

## Why it matters

The practice could speed deportations by making last-minute legal challenges harder to pursue, immigration lawyers said. If you cannot locate a client in ICE custody, you cannot:

- Verify their detention status or location
- File emergency motions or stays of removal
- Coordinate with family members
- Prepare for removal proceedings
- Respond to urgent detention or health issues
- Meet attorney–client communication obligations

Families of detainees "are freaking out because they think the person has been deported and they don't know where the person might be," and the new policy has "serious implications" for attorneys' ability to make contact with their clients.

The scale is significant: Nearly 16,000 people who were arrested and booked into ICE detention in July were subject to final removal orders, more than a third of the overall bookings.

## Way forward

- **Check the ICE locator immediately** if you represent anyone with a final removal order. If they no longer appear, assume they may have been removed from the public system. Call the facility directly or ICE's field office to verify location and status.

- **Request facility information directly.** If you cannot find a detainee using the online system, contact an ICE Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO) field office. If you know the facility where the person is being held, contact the immigration detention facility directly.

- **Preserve your right to emergency contact.** Keep detailed client intake information (facility name, booking number, phone contact) in case the public system fails. Document all attempts to locate your client.

- **Monitor court-ordered exceptions.** Clients held in Adelanto, Minneapolis, New York City, or suburban Chicago *should* remain visible in the locator system because judges have ordered prompt listing. If they disappear from those facilities, report it to the court or the litigation team overseeing the case.
