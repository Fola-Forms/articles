USCIS has issued revised guidance on how to verify and process Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for Ethiopia in the SAVE system, superseding an earlier July 30 announcement. A U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts issued an order on January 30, 2026, staying the TPS Ethiopia termination. This update reflects new EAD expiration dates following that court order.

## What changed

This guidance supersedes the SAVE Termination of TPS for Ethiopia message posted on July 30, 2026. TPS Ethiopia beneficiaries will keep their status and employment authorization, and their documentation will remain valid per the court order, with Forms I-766 (Employment Authorization Documents with category A12 or C19) remaining valid and extended.

The key update affects two elements:

**EAD expiration dates.** USCIS revised the employment authorized through date from August 6, 2026, to August 19, 2026. Practitioners should refer to the EAD Extension dropdown on the TPS Ethiopia webpage for a full list of which original EAD expiration dates are extended pending the resolution of the litigation.

**SAVE verification response.** TPS beneficiaries will receive a SAVE manual response of "Temporary Protected Status – Employment Authorized - Temp Emp Auth," with the employment authorized through date being the EAD expiration date of August 19, 2026, and DHS comments stating "TPS and employment authorization are extended per court order."

## Why it matters

Ethiopian TPS beneficiaries live under ongoing litigation uncertainty. The extension is limited relief until the lower courts align with the U.S. Supreme Court's favorable decision in Mullin v. Doe, 609 U.S. ____ (2026), issued on June 25, 2026. Because the Supreme Court has already ruled in favor of the government in TPS termination cases, the temporary stay may not hold long-term.

For employers and practitioners: you now have a three-week window (through August 19) to update I-9s and employment records with the revised EAD dates. SAVE verification procedures have shifted, so employers using automated systems should refresh their understanding of how the manual response language will appear. Clients with EADs expiring between August 6 and August 19 should not attempt to file renewal applications until there is final clarity on TPS status — filing prematurely could trigger additional scrutiny or denial risk if the stay is lifted.

## Way forward

- **Check the USCIS TPS Ethiopia page regularly.** USCIS notes this matter is in active litigation, so the status of the individual's TPS and employment authorization may change. Bookmark https://www.uscis.gov/humanitarian/temporary-protected-status/temporary-protected-status-designated-country-ethiopia and review it weekly.

- **Update I-9 and SAVE records.** If you represent employers or have clients in the hire/re-verify cycle, correct any Forms I-9 that show the old August 6 date to August 19, 2026. Coordinate with your HR contacts to ensure SAVE manual responses are interpreted correctly.

- **Advise clients to stay put.** Unless a client has another eligible immigration pathway (family petition, employment sponsorship, asylum), counsel them not to file new applications until the litigation settles. A premature filing could complicate TPS status later.

- **Plan for September.** Given the Supreme Court's June 2026 ruling and the temporary nature of this extension, begin discussing contingency options with Ethiopian TPS clients now—whether that's exploring other status options, updating I-140 employment petitions, or preparing departure plans if the stay does not hold.
