The employment-based green card pipeline is under unprecedented strain. A new report from the American Immigration Council analyzing USCIS data from fiscal years 2016 through 2025 documents record I-140 backlogs, rising denial rates, growing visa wait times, and longer processing delays across the employment-based green card system. For practitioners and employers, this means tighter timelines, higher scrutiny, and less predictable outcomes on cases you're advising today.

## What changed

The American Immigration Council's new USCIS Filing Trends Dashboard examined trends in employment-based immigration filings, processing times, backlogs, visa availability, denial rates, and related benefits between fiscal year FY2016 and FY2025.

The data shows material deterioration on multiple fronts:

- **Form I-485 processing:** Although the backlog clearance times of Form I-485 improved dramatically after their pandemic-era peak of 42.1 months in FY2021 Q2, they climbed back to roughly 17 months throughout FY2025.

- **Denial rates:** The average denial rate during the first three quarters of the second Trump administration (11.8 percent) was notably higher than the decade-long average of 8.0 percent.

- **Systemic capacity:** Demand for immigrant workers has continued to rise, but USCIS processing capacity has not kept pace.

## Why it matters

Rising backlogs, heightened scrutiny in some categories, and persistent visa-number constraints are creating longer and less predictable timelines for both employers and immigrants, with significant implications for workforce planning and the United States' ability to attract and retain global talent.

For you as a practitioner, this shifts your client communication on three fronts:

1. **Timeline counseling:** A 17-month I-485 processing window is substantially longer than historical norms. Advise clients that adjustment of status will require patience; concurrent I-140/I-485 filing may be strategically important to compress overall timeline where eligible.

2. **Denial risk:** The 11.8% denial rate under the current administration is 48% higher than the 8.0% decade average. This affects how you scope RFE responses, position credentials, and manage client expectations. Request evidence packages should be more comprehensive and precise.

3. **Employment verification:** Form I-131/I-765 delays correlate with I-485 backlog. Employers relying on immediate work authorization should plan for extended delays in EAD issuance.

## Way forward

- **Review your pending I-485 cases:** For cases filed in the past 12 months, re-evaluate whether an expedite request on humanitarian grounds (hardship affidavit) is defensible. Prepare clients for 17-month floors, not 12-month processing windows.

- **Strengthen I-140 evidence:** With denial rates elevated, ensure petitions include comprehensive evidence of job duties, wage data, and labor market conditions from the outset. Do not rely on RFE responses to fill gaps.

- **Plan visa availability:** Work with employer clients on visa bulletin tracking and per-country cap implications for India, China, and Philippines-born beneficiaries. Visa number availability remains volatile.

- **Monitor for further policy shifts:** The report covers only through Q3 FY2025. Check the [USCIS Filing Trends Dashboard](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/report/uscis-employment-based-immigration/) for ongoing updates to denial rates and processing times.
