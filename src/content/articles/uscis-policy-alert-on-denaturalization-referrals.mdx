USCIS issued a policy alert on revocation of naturalization (denaturalization) under section 340 of the Immigration and Nationality Act, effective immediately. The updated guidance reorganizes and expands the agency's instructions on when naturalization may be revoked, how potential cases are identified and which cases may be prioritized for referral.

## What changed

The revisions update Volume 12 of the USCIS Policy Manual, which covers citizenship and naturalization, including Part L, "Loss of U.S. Nationality and Revocation of Naturalization". The policy affirms that for any pending or future filed petition or application associated with a U.S. citizen, USCIS determines whether the petitioner lawfully obtained citizenship.

USCIS said the changes do not alter the underlying legal grounds for denaturalization. Instead, they clarify existing guidance on the agency's referral and review process. U.S. citizenship obtained through naturalization may be subject to revocation when it was procured illegally or through willful misrepresentation or concealment of a material fact.

The policy provides reorganized and expanded instructions on denaturalization case identification and prioritization for referral to the Department of Justice.

## Why it matters

For practitioners, this guidance directly affects how you advise naturalized clients on potential vulnerabilities. When a naturalized U.S. citizen files any petition or application with USCIS, the agency will now systematically determine whether that person lawfully obtained citizenship. This applies to any immigration benefit petition or application filed by or on behalf of a naturalized citizen—including spousal petitions, employment-based applications, consular processing, and derivative benefits.

The clarified prioritization criteria give USCIS structural guidance on which denaturalization candidates to refer to DOJ. Practitioners should be aware that USCIS will review the naturalization file and underlying background materials during the adjudication of any benefit application filed by a naturalized citizen.

The policy does not itself revoke the citizenship of any naturalized American. A USCIS referral, investigation or designation for priority review is not the same as a judicial revocation. Actual denaturalization requires the applicable legal proceeding and the government to meet the required burden of proof.

## Way forward

- **Review client files.** If you represent a naturalized client seeking any immigration benefit, obtain and review the naturalization file and N-400 application now to identify any potential inconsistencies or misrepresentations that USCIS might flag.

- **Disclose proactively.** If you discover discrepancies in a client's naturalization application (minor errors, omissions, or correctable inconsistencies), consider early disclosure and correction through appropriate channels before filing a new benefit application.

- **Know the judicial standard.** Understand that denaturalization still requires a judicial proceeding and clear and convincing evidence of willful misrepresentation or concealment of a material fact. A USCIS referral is not a revocation.

- **Provide plain-language counsel.** Advise naturalized clients that when they file immigration applications or petitions, USCIS will review whether their naturalization was lawfully obtained as part of the adjudication process.
