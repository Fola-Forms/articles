On September 14, 2026, one day before the rule was set to take effect, Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV of the District of Massachusetts postponed the effective date of the Department of Homeland Security's (DHS's) Final Rule replacing duration of status with fixed periods of admission for F, J, and I nonimmigrants (international students). The court issued nationwide preliminary relief, affecting the framework for all F-1 students, J-1 exchange visitors, and I media representatives across the country.

## What changed

The Final Rule, published in the Federal Register on July 17, 2026, would have ended a framework that has governed F admissions since 1978 and J admissions since 1985, replacing it with a system under which F and J nonimmigrants would be admitted for a fixed period of four years or the program end date, whichever came first. I visa holders (foreign media representatives) would have been limited to 240-day admission periods.

The case is Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, No. 1:26-cv-13799-FDS (D. Mass. Sept. 14, 2026). The court relief is nationwide, and reaches the whole rule. The court found that the plaintiffs had demonstrated a substantial likelihood of success on the merits of their Administrative Procedure Act (APA) claim, that immediate irreparable harm would follow absent relief, and that the balance of equities and the public interest favored the issuing of an injunction.

The rule is postponed, not vacated, and the case continues on the merits.

## Why it matters

For practitioners, the bottom line is straightforward: duration of status remains the operative framework for F, J, and I nonimmigrants, for now. This means you do not advise your student-visa clients or school/exchange-visitor clients to prepare for fixed admission end dates, extension-of-stay filings with USCIS, or restrictions on school transfers and changes in academic objectives that the rule would have imposed.

The lawsuit was filed by a coalition including NAFSA, the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration, the American Federation of Teachers, and the UAW, alleging that the fixed admission regulation violates the APA because DHS provided an inadequate comment period, failed to adequately assess the costs and benefits of the regulation, and gave insufficient consideration to public comments; the plaintiffs also contend that the regulation is arbitrary and capricious and exceeds DHS's statutory authority.

The litigation is ongoing. The government is likely to quickly appeal the court decision. Until the merits are resolved or the preliminary injunction is lifted, the existing duration-of-status framework controls.

## Way forward

- **Pause transition planning.** If you represent schools, exchange-visitor programs, or employers, do not implement fixed admission workflows or systems architecture premised on a September 15, 2026 transition.

- **Maintain D/S compliance.** Continue advising clients that F, J, and I nonimmigrants must maintain the terms and conditions of their programs (course of study, exchange activity, authorized employment, etc.) and report changes through SEVIS.

- **Monitor the docket.** Check the District of Massachusetts website for the Presidents' Alliance case (D. Mass. 1:26-cv-13799-FDS) and watch for DHS's response, appeal, or motion to stay the preliminary injunction. Policy can shift rapidly pending the government's next move.

- **Verify current agency guidance.** Confirm with DHS, SEVIS, or the Department of State/exchange visitor program office how the preliminary injunction affects any internal guidance or instructions issued in preparation for the September 15 effective date.
