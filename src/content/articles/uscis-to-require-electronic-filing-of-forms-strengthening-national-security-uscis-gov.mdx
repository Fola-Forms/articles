DHS has introduced an interim final rule (IFR) that allows USCIS to mandate electronic filing for certain immigration forms. Under this IFR, USCIS may require future filings of any immigration form that has been available for e-filing for at least 180 days to be submitted online. The rule becomes effective August 11, 2026, with a public comment period extending through October 13, 2026.

## What changed

The Department of Homeland Security is introducing an interim final rule that allows USCIS to mandate electronic filing for certain immigration forms. Key provisions include:

- USCIS may require future filings of any immigration form that has been available for e-filing for at least 180 days to be submitted online.
- USCIS will notify the public on its website at least 60 days before requiring e-filing of a form.
- Filers must use an online account to submit an immigration form to USCIS, either by completing the form online or by uploading a PDF of the completed form and related evidence.

The Interim Final Rule is effective on Aug. 11, 2026, and DHS will consider public comments submitted on or before Oct. 13, 2026. This is not an immediate mandate for all forms — USCIS must identify which forms will transition to mandatory e-filing and provide practitioners and applicants with at least 60 days' notice before enforcement begins.

## Why it matters

This interim final rule represents a structural shift in USCIS filing infrastructure. This initiative will eliminate reliance on the U.S. Department of the Treasury's physical lockbox services and accelerate the shift to electronic fee payments.

For immigration practitioners and their clients, the implications are significant:

- **Filing timeline planning**: Once a form transitions to mandatory e-filing, paper filings will no longer be accepted. You will need to counsel clients on the mechanics of e-filing — either guided online completion or PDF upload — well in advance.
- **Intake workflows**: Your client intake and document-collection procedures may need to change to accommodate digital-only submissions. Systems currently built around paper filing and lockbox timelines will require adjustment.
- **Notice and transition period**: Because USCIS must provide 60 days' advance notice, monitor the USCIS website closely for announcements of which forms are being converted. This gives you a defined window to retrain staff and update client advisories.

The transition should deliver substantial cost savings and greater efficiency for the federal government. The rule does not immediately eliminate paper filing but creates the framework for USCIS to do so form-by-form.

## Way forward

- **Watch the USCIS website**: USCIS will notify the public on its website at least 60 days before requiring e-filing of a form. Bookmark [https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts) and check for form-specific transition announcements.
- **Review e-filing capabilities now**: Familiarize yourself and your team with the e-filing process for forms already available online. Practice guided completion and PDF upload workflows before they become mandatory.
- **Update client engagement letters and fee agreements**: Clarify that certain forms may require electronic submission and that you (or the client, if self-represented) will need online USCIS accounts and internet access to file.
- **Consider submitting comments**: DHS will consider public comments submitted on or before Oct. 13, 2026. If you have concerns about the transition timeline or specific forms, submit feedback to the Federal eRulemaking Portal at [https://www.regulations.gov](https://www.regulations.gov) referencing this interim final rule.
