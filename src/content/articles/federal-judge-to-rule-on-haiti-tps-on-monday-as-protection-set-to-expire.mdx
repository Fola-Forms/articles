A federal judge is set to rule on Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for hundreds of thousands of Haitians on Monday, the protection set to expire. The judge on Friday temporarily delayed the end of TPS for Haitians, which had been scheduled to expire that day. The ruling affects approximately 350,000 Haitians who rely on TPS.

## What changed

The court order keeps protections in place through Monday, July 27, when a judge is expected to decide when the program should officially end. This temporary extension follows prior court action and represents the most recent halt in TPS termination for Haiti. A district judge on Feb. 2 blocked the termination as the case moved ultimately to the Supreme Court, which ruled June 25 that the administration can end the program for Haitians and Syrians. Since that 6-3 TPS ruling, Haiti's TPS status has stayed in effect through a series of short administrative extensions — first to July 10, then to July 24 — while the case has moved back down from the Supreme Court to the lower courts.

## Why it matters

For the 350,000 Haitians with TPS, this temporary stay preserves work authorization and protection from deportation while litigation continues. Immigration attorneys are advising TPS recipients to prepare for the possibility that protections could end. Vanessa Joseph with Catholic Legal Services encouraged families to gather important documents and make plans for their finances, businesses, and children. The fluid legal landscape means practitioners must advise clients on contingency planning while the courts determine the ultimate outcome. The Trump administration has argued that conditions in Haiti no longer justify the designation, despite a State Department advisory warning Americans against traveling to the Caribbean nation because of risks associated with "crime, kidnapping, terrorism, unrest, and limited health care."

## Way forward

- **Monitor the Monday ruling closely.** Expect a federal court decision on whether and when TPS for Haiti should terminate. Subscribe to USCIS alerts and check the case docket (Miot et al. v. Trump et al., No. 25-cv-02471-ACR, D.D.C.) for the order.
  
- **Advise clients on alternative pathways.** Immigration attorneys are advising TPS recipients to prepare for the possibility that protections could end. Review each beneficiary's eligibility for asylum, cancellation of removal, U visas, or other status options.

- **Document financial, family, and business stakes.** Encourage clients to organize evidence of ties to the U.S. (employment, property, family relationships, community contributions) in case removal proceedings become necessary.

- **Track legislative efforts.** Republican members of Congress from South Florida have urged the Trump administration to extend the program. If eventually approved by the Senate, the legislation would extend TPS protections for Haitians through April 2029.
