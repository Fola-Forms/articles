A federal judge has ruled that 24 people held at the Cascade County Detention Center were arrested by immigration officers without the legal steps required to keep them in custody, and they have been released. U.S. District Judge Brian Morris allowed the detainees' case to proceed as a class action, finding they shared a central claim about warrantless arrests. This ruling creates a blueprint for challenging similar arrests across the district and expands the window for detained immigrants to seek release based on arrest lawfulness.

## What changed

Judge Morris certified a class action, with federal authorities identifying 53 people who met the class criteria. Twenty-four people were granted release on Friday, and counsel estimates around 39 people total will be released by the end of the week, including five who secured release earlier through other means.

The legal standard for a warrantless arrest by immigration officers requires reason to believe the person is violating immigration law and is likely to escape before a warrant can be obtained. The judge found that ICE failed to satisfy this standard in the class members' arrests.

The class action ruling did not automatically free everyone; their attorneys must ask the court to release each person individually. The ruling does not settle anyone's underlying immigration case—many of those released will still go through immigration proceedings while living outside a jail.

## Why it matters

This decision establishes a factual and legal template for challenging ICE warrantless arrests within the District of Montana. Immigration practitioners with clients detained following similar arrests now have a precedent showing that absent particularized evidence of flight risk and immigration law violation *at the time of arrest*, the arrest itself may be unlawful and subject the detainee to habeas relief.

The class action structure is significant: rather than litigating 53 separate cases, the attorney can move for individual release orders under the framework the judge has already endorsed. This reduces both litigation cost and delay for detainees.

The timing of the order helped attorneys get the 24 people home before they could be moved to other facilities—a practical reminder that once a detainee is transferred out of state, habeas jurisdiction may shift and release becomes harder to secure.

## Way forward

- **If your client was arrested by ICE in Montana without a warrant:** Review the arrest report and testimony for evidence of (a) reasonable belief of immigration law violation and (b) likelihood of escape. If either is absent or weak, file a habeas corpus petition citing this class action.

- **If your client is part of or eligible for the 53-person class:** Contact the plaintiff's counsel (Upper Seven Law is identified in reporting) to determine if your client meets the criteria and whether a motion for individual release is pending.

- **Monitor the docket:** Releases are expected to continue through the end of the week. Track the judge's individual release orders to understand which factual findings favor release and which do not.

- **Prepare for ongoing immigration proceedings:** Release from detention does not end the immigration case. Secured release should be followed immediately by preparation of the merits defense (asylum, cancellation of removal, etc.).
