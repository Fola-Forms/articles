The U.S. Department of State has proposed significant changes to the Exchange Visitor Program (EVP) that would expand the circumstances under which J-1 exchange visitors can have their programs terminated. The proposal was published as a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking and is scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on July 30, 2026, with a 60-day public comment period. The changes are intended to strengthen program integrity, visitor welfare, and national security while modernizing regulations that have remained largely unchanged since 1999.

## What changed

The rule would amend regulations governing J-1 exchange visitors and J-2 dependents, including program termination, program extensions, and reinstatement procedures.

**Mandatory termination grounds for sponsors.** Under the proposed rule, a J-1 sponsor must terminate an exchange visitor's participation in their cultural exchange program if the foreign national fails to maintain the health and accident insurance coverage that is required for the J-1 and their dependent J-2 family members under State Department regulations. Under the current regulation, a J-1 sponsor must terminate an exchange visitor's participation only if the failure to maintain required health and accident insurance is deemed willful. The proposed rule removes the "willful" qualifier. 

The State Department also adds as a mandatory sponsor termination ground a foreign national's falsification or failure to provide full and truthful information and documentation in their application or ongoing program.

**DOS discretionary termination authority.** The proposal would expand the Department of State's discretionary authority to terminate exchange visitor participation. Under the proposed regulation, the Department could terminate a participant's exchange program when: The Department of State revokes or cancels a visa with immediate effect; or The Department of Homeland Security cancels a visa with immediate effect. The Department states that in such situations, affected individuals could be required to leave the United States immediately or risk removal proceedings.

**Program extensions and reinstatement.** The proposed replacement of 22 CFR 62.43 (Extension of Program) streamlines and clarifies the processes for extensions, both within and beyond the maximum program duration, by integrating these procedures with SEVIS functionalities. One notable change would eliminate a separate extension provision that has historically applied only to au pair participants, bringing those cases under the same general extension rules used for other exchange visitors. The proposed rule also addresses reinstatement to valid program status.

## Why it matters

If finalized, this proposal fundamentally reshapes how sponsors and the State Department manage J-1 program termination, making it easier to terminate without showing intent or willfulness. The removal of the "willful" requirement for insurance violations means even inadvertent lapses in health coverage—whether a clerical error, a gap in coverage timing, or incomplete documentation—now trigger automatic termination by the sponsor.

The addition of falsification or incomplete information as mandatory grounds creates a broad new basis for termination applicable to any misstatement in the initial application or during ongoing program participation. This significantly raises the compliance bar for participants and sponsors alike.

The new discretionary authority for DOS to terminate when *any* visa is revoked or canceled by either DOS or DHS effectively ties J-1 status to visa validity in a way not currently explicit in regulation. Combined with other removal-related changes in DHS rules taking effect September 15, 2026, this creates a tighter enforcement ecosystem for exchange visitors.

For practitioners advising sponsors, the streamlined SEVIS-integrated extension procedures may reduce paperwork, but the expanded termination grounds materially increase the risk profile for clients. For those advising J-1 visa holders, the proposal creates new jeopardy points—particularly around health insurance compliance and application disclosures.

## Way forward

- **Review client files now.** If you represent exchange visitor program sponsors, audit SEVIS records and insurance documentation for all current J-1 and J-2 participants. Ensure health coverage meets current requirements and that all paperwork is complete and truthful.

- **Prepare comment submissions.** Public comments on the proposal will be accepted for 60 days from today's publication in the Federal Register. Organizations managing exchange programs should consider submitting substantive comments on the "willful" removal, the breadth of falsification grounds, and any operational or compliance concerns.

- **Monitor visa status closely.** Under the proposed DOS discretionary authority, coordinate closely with visa services and immigration counsel if any client receives notice of visa revocation or cancellation to understand J-1 termination implications.

- **Align with new F/J/I rules.** The proposed rule is designed to align exchange visitor program rules with the new F/J/I nonimmigrant authorized stay regulation set to take effect on September 15, 2026. Ensure your practice is prepared for the fixed-period-of-admission requirement that replaces Duration of Status for J-1 visitors.
