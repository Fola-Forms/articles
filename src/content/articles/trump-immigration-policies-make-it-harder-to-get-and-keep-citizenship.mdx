The Trump administration has implemented a series of policies making U.S. citizenship substantially harder to acquire and easier to revoke. For immigration practitioners, these changes require immediate adjustments to how you counsel and strategize naturalization cases.

## What changed

USCIS has made the civics test harder to pass, denied applications at a higher rate and proposed increasing the cost of applying for citizenship. More specifically:

- **Civics exam**: The test has been made more difficult to pass, affecting how applicants prepare.
- **Fee hikes**: USCIS has proposed significant fee increases up to 80% and eliminated hardship waivers.
- **Denial rates**: Naturalization denial rates have more than doubled.
- **Processing delays**: Application processing has plummeted by 75%, leading to a growing backlog despite fewer applications.
- **Denaturalization enforcement**: The Department of Justice has prioritized denaturalization, aiming for 100–200 cases monthly, a major increase.

## Why it matters

These changes reshape the economics and timeline of naturalization practice:

**Cost and affordability**: An 80% fee increase combined with elimination of hardship waivers means fewer low-income applicants can afford to file. Clients already approved for green cards will face sticker shock and may delay or abandon applications.

**Preparation burden**: A harder civics exam requires more intensive client preparation. You'll need to set realistic expectations about passage rates and may face more RFEs or denials on cultural or knowledge grounds.

**Processing paralysis**: A 75% drop in processing speed, despite lower volume, signals severe backlog. If a client has time-sensitive concerns (voting eligibility, employment-based green card renewal tied to I-131 travel authorization, removal proceedings pending), the timeline becomes critical.

**Denaturalization risk**: A tenfold increase in DOJ denaturalization actions means practitioners must screen existing citizens for potential vulnerabilities—prior fraud, criminal issues, or faulty naturalizations—before advising on family-based petitions or other matters where the client's status will be scrutinized.

**Compounding effect**: These policies work together. Higher denial rates + slower processing + higher fees = reduced client base seeking citizenship, while simultaneous denaturalization enforcement creates fear and uncertainty among the naturalization-eligible population.

## Way forward

- **Audit your docket**: Review pending N-400 cases for civics-test readiness. Invest in intensive civics preparation materials and mock exams; passage rates have compressed.
- **Fee planning**: Advise green-card holders on the timing of N-400 filing before further fee increases take effect. Explore fee-waiver eligibility strictly; waivers are no longer available for hardship.
- **Denaturalization screening**: For clients with existing citizenship or seeking to sponsor family members as future citizens, conduct a detailed intake on the naturalization circumstances (prior fraud, criminal conviction after naturalization, false statements, etc.). Flag vulnerabilities early.
- **Timeline reset**: In planning family-based or employment-based cases, assume N-400 processing will take significantly longer. Build in contingency time for delays and potential denials that may require refiling or appeals.
