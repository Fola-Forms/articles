## What changed

USCIS continues to maintain its policy guidance on F and M nonimmigrant students in [USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 2, Part F](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-f), which consolidates the operating rules for academic students. That guidance — together with the underlying regulation at [8 CFR 214.2(f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2#p-214.2(f)) and consular procedure in [9 FAM 402.5-5](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040205.html) — sets the sequence every initial F-1 applicant follows: school admission, a Form I-20 generated in SEVIS by a Designated School Official, payment of the I-901 SEVIS fee, a consular DS-160 application and interview, and physical entry to the United States within a fixed window before the program start date.

The window is the part students most often misread. Under [8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2#p-214.2(f)(5)(i)), an F-1 may be admitted no earlier than 30 days before the program start date listed on the I-20. CBP enforces that limit at the port of entry; arrivals on day 31 or earlier are routinely deferred or denied entry in F-1.

## Why it matters

The 30-day rule and the SEVIS-driven document chain are not paperwork rituals — they determine whether an F-1 admission is lawful from the first minute of status. Three concrete consequences:

1. **A premature arrival voids the F-1 admission.** CBP can refuse entry, send the traveler to secondary, or admit on a different status (typically B-2 with annotation, if at all). A B-2-then-change-of-status workaround forfeits the simpler F-1 path and forces a Form I-539 filing. USCIS describes the change-of-status mechanics in [Policy Manual Vol 2, Part A, Chapter 4](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-a-chapter-4).

2. **A defective I-20 voids the visa application.** Consular officers cross-check the I-20 against SEVIS in real time. A school that has not completed SEVP recertification, a Designated School Official who has not signed page 1, or a SEVIS ID mismatch will all stop the interview. Schools' DSO duties are codified at [8 CFR 214.3(g)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.3#p-214.3(g)).

3. **The I-901 SEVIS fee is a precondition to issuance.** The student pays the I-901 fee at [FMJfee.com](https://www.fmjfee.com/i901fee/index.html) before the visa interview; the receipt is presented at the consulate and again at the port of entry. The fee is set under [8 CFR 214.13](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.13) and administered by SEVP via the [Study in the States I-901 page](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/prepare/paying-the-i-901-sevis-fee).

The downstream effect of getting these three right is that the student arrives with "Duration of Status" (D/S) noted on the I-94 — meaning the F-1 is authorized for the entire course of study plus the applicable grace period, without a fixed expiration date. That status framework is summarised in 9 FAM 402.5-5.

## Way forward

A clean initial F-1 issuance moves in this order. Each step has a hard dependency on the prior one — skipping or reordering is the most common source of refusal.

**1. Admission and I-20 generation.** The student is admitted to a SEVP-certified school. A Designated School Official creates the [Form I-20](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/i-20) in SEVIS, populating the program start date, end date, program of study, and source-of-funds attestation. The DSO and the student both sign page 1.

**2. I-901 SEVIS fee payment.** The student pays the I-901 fee at [FMJfee.com](https://www.fmjfee.com/i901fee/index.html). The receipt is typically required electronically at the DS-160 stage and physically at the visa interview.

**3. DS-160 and consular interview.** The student files the [DS-160 online nonimmigrant visa application](https://ceac.state.gov/genniv/) and schedules the interview at the U.S. embassy or consulate with jurisdiction over their place of residence. The interview follows 9 FAM 402.5-5; the officer evaluates whether the applicant has overcome the [INA §214(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1184&num=0&edition=prelim) presumption of immigrant intent. Approved applicants receive the F-1 visa foil in the passport, typically within a week.

**4. Port of entry — observe the 30-day rule.** The student may not enter the United States earlier than 30 days before the program start date on the I-20. Counting is calendar days, inclusive of weekends and holidays. CBP admits the student in F-1 D/S status; the [I-94 admission record](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/) is generated electronically and should be downloaded and saved.

**5. SEVIS registration at the school.** Once on campus, the student must report to the DSO so the school can register the SEVIS record as "Active" — the regulation requires this no later than 30 days after the program start date, per [8 CFR 214.3(g)(3)(iii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.3#p-214.3(g)(3)(iii)). Failure to register terminates the SEVIS record and unwinds the F-1 status.

A few high-frequency edge cases worth flagging up front:

- **Visa-exempt nationals (Canadians, Bermudians)** do not need a visa foil but still need the I-20 and the I-901 receipt at the port of entry. The exemption is covered in [9 FAM 402.5-5(M)](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040205.html).
- **Initial vs. transfer vs. change-of-program I-20s** carry different SEVIS record codes; a student transferring schools must coordinate the SEVIS release date between old and new DSOs under [8 CFR 214.2(f)(8)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2#p-214.2(f)(8)).
- **Spouses and minor children** travel on F-2 visas issued under derivative I-20s; F-2 dependents have their own activity restrictions covered in a separate article in this series.
