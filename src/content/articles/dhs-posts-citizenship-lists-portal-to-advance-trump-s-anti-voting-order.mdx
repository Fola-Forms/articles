DHS is "on track" to finish building out a portal by June 30 that will allow state election officials to access lists of verified U.S. citizens eligible to vote in each state. The agency has published implementation plans for a two-phase rollout of the "Citizenship Lists Portal," which consolidates naturalization and immigration data to create citizenship verification records for state elections officials—before individual citizens can audit the records for accuracy.

## What changed

In the first phase, state election officials will be allowed to access separate citizenship lists via the portal using data from USCIS, SSA and DOS, with each list including citizenship data that includes "name, date of birth, and relevant agency identifiers." State election officials will be provided access to a portal via Login.gov and will be able to download separate outputs from USCIS, SSA, and DOS, subject to agency-specific terms of service and disclaimers.

Individual citizens will eventually be able to use the portal to look up their own voting records and "submit corrections" some time later this year, but that this won't be possible by June 30th.

## Why it matters

The timing creates a critical gap: state election officials will have access to federal citizenship data 60 days before federal elections, while the public portal for citizens to verify their own records remains unavailable at launch. The databases DHS and SSA would pull from to compile the lists are notoriously inaccurate when used to determine voter eligibility. DHS itself admitted that these lists will contain widespread inaccuracies that could result in eligible voters being erroneously removed from state voter rolls just weeks before Election Day and disenfranchised.

For immigration practitioners, this matters because USCIS naturalization records and data from the agency's SAVE system will feed directly into these citizenship lists. Naturalization applicants and recent naturalized citizens should expect their records to be cross-checked against state voter rolls using this federal data.

## Way forward

- **Review client records**: If you represent naturalization applicants or recent clients, verify that their USCIS and SSA records are accurate. Any mismatches in name, date of birth, or citizenship status flags now could cascade into voter-roll removals later.
- **Audit SAVE discrepancies**: Check for any pending issues in the SAVE system that may cause your client to be excluded from the citizenship lists.
- **Monitor state election responses**: Track which states begin using the portal and whether they implement additional verification requirements that could affect your clients' voting eligibility or prompt document requests.
- **Stay informed on litigation**: Multiple lawsuits are challenging the legality of this system. Monitor federal court decisions, as injunctions could alter implementation timelines.
