A USCIS Notice of Intent to Revoke (NOIR) tells a petitioner that the agency, having previously approved a petition, now intends to undo that approval. The statutory authority comes from [**INA §205**](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1155&num=0&edition=prelim) (8 U.S.C. §1155), which lets the Secretary of Homeland Security revoke approval "at any time, for what he deems to be good and sufficient cause." The implementing regulation is [**8 CFR §205.2**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-205.2). The BIA framework that governs the merits is [**Matter of Estime, 19 I&N Dec. 450 (BIA 1987)**](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/07/25/3060.pdf), reaffirmed in [**Matter of Arias, 19 I&N Dec. 568 (BIA 1988)**](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/07/25/3084.pdf), and refined for marriage-based revocations by [**Matter of P. Singh, 27 I&N Dec. 598 (BIA 2019)**](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/page/file/1185776/download), decided July 11, 2019.

## What changed

[**8 CFR §205.2(a)**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-205.2) gives a USCIS director authority to revoke approval "if the necessity for revocation comes to the attention of [USCIS]." Before revoking, [§205.2(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-205.2) requires written notice with "specific reasons" and an opportunity to "offer evidence in support of the petition and in opposition to the grounds alleged for revocation." Under [**8 CFR §205.2(c)**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-205.2), the petitioner has **30 days** from service of the NOIR to respond — three days longer if the notice is mailed, per [8 CFR §103.8(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.8).

[**Matter of Estime**](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/07/25/3060.pdf) defines the standard: an NOIR must, "when issued, contain a specific statement of the facts underlying the proposed action" and constitute "good and sufficient cause for the revocation of [the] approval." A NOIR that is conclusory — "we now believe the marriage was not bona fide" without identifying which facts support that view — is procedurally deficient and a basis for reversal on appeal. [**Matter of Arias**](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/07/25/3084.pdf) added that the evidence in the record at the time of approval cannot, by itself, be re-weighed as grounds for revocation — there must be new information or a re-examination of evidence that meets the **substantial-and-probative evidence** threshold the BIA articulated in [**Matter of Tawfik, 20 I&N Dec. 166 (BIA 1990)**](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/07/25/3147.pdf) and [**Matter of P. Singh**](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/page/file/1185776/download).

The 2019 [P. Singh](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/page/file/1185776/download) decision is the most consequential recent development. It clarified that, in marriage-fraud revocations, [INA §204(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1154&num=0&edition=prelim) — the lifetime marriage-fraud bar — requires "substantial and probative evidence" of fraud in the record before approval can be revoked on that basis. A bare divorce, a short marriage, or a single inconsistent statement is not enough.

## Why it matters

NOIRs hit later and hurt more than RFEs or NOIDs. The approval the petitioner is defending may already have produced a [priority date](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin.html), an [AC21 H-1B extension](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-h-chapter-9), or [I-140 portability rights under AC21 §106(c)](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-e-chapter-5) — all of which are tied to the approved petition. A revocation can unwind years of downstream planning.

Three rules drive the defense:

**Portability survives revocation only for narrow reasons.** Under [AC21 §106(c)](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-e-chapter-5) (codified at [INA §204(j)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1154&num=0&edition=prelim)), an I-140 remains valid for AC21 portability if it was approved and has been so for at least 180 days, **unless** revoked for fraud, willful misrepresentation, [INA §204(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1154&num=0&edition=prelim) marriage-fraud bar, agency material error, or revocation on notice for any of those grounds. A revocation for **business cessation alone** does not destroy portability — see [USCIS PM Vol 7, Part E, Chapter 5](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-e-chapter-5). The NOIR response should attack the ground, not just the revocation: if USCIS proposes to revoke for fraud, the response must show no fraud; if it proposes to revoke for business cessation, the response should concede portability rights are preserved while contesting the cessation finding.

**Bona fides reach back to the date of approval.** In an I-130 NOIR, the agency must show the marriage was not bona fide **at inception**. A genuine marriage that later fails is not [§204(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1154&num=0&edition=prelim) fraud. The response packages contemporaneous evidence from the marriage period: joint leases, joint accounts opened at the relevant time, photographs with dated metadata, declarations from family and friends who knew the couple then. Post-divorce affidavits are weaker but still useful when the record needs context.

**Derogatory information must be disclosed.** [8 CFR §103.2(b)(16)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.2#p-103.2(b)(16)(i)) requires USCIS to disclose derogatory information "of which the applicant or petitioner is unaware" before relying on it. If the NOIR references an [FDNS site visit](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/organization/directorates-and-program-offices/fraud-detection-and-national-security-directorate), an unidentified informant, or a "review of A-file information" without specifics, the response should demand particulars in writing and preserve the procedural objection for [AAO appeal](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/organization/directorates-and-program-offices/administrative-appeals-office-aao) or federal-court [APA review](https://www.justice.gov/jm/civil-resource-manual-216-administrative-procedure-act).

## Way forward

- **Calendar 30 days plus mailing time, then work backward.** [8 CFR §205.2(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-205.2) and [§103.8(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.8) are rigid. Build a 5-day buffer for FedEx delivery and confirm receipt with tracking.
- **Demand the file under FOIA in parallel.** A [Form G-639 FOIA request](https://www.uscis.gov/g-639) to USCIS for the underlying A-file often surfaces the [FDNS report](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/organization/directorates-and-program-offices/fraud-detection-and-national-security-directorate) or interview summary that drove the NOIR — invaluable for the response. Track-3 FOIA processing returns in days to weeks; ordinary processing can take months.
- **Quote Estime and Tawfik in the response.** Force the officer to engage with the actual standard. A NOIR that recites "we have reason to believe" without specific underlying facts fails [Matter of Estime](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/07/25/3060.pdf); say so in writing.
- **Address the **specific** ground, not the **kind** of ground.** A fraud NOIR demands a fraud rebuttal. A cessation NOIR demands a business-existence rebuttal. Conflating them weakens both.
- **Preserve a [Form I-290B](https://www.uscis.gov/i-290b) appeal.** If revocation issues, the [AAO](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/organization/directorates-and-program-offices/administrative-appeals-office-aao) has jurisdiction over [I-140](https://www.uscis.gov/i-140) revocations under [8 CFR §103.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/section-103.3); the [BIA](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/board-of-immigration-appeals) hears [I-130](https://www.uscis.gov/i-130) appeals under [8 CFR §1003.1(b)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-V/section-1003.1#p-1003.1(b)(5)). The 30-day appeal deadline runs from the revocation decision, not the NOIR.
- **Coordinate with the beneficiary's separate filings.** A pending [I-485](https://www.uscis.gov/i-485) or [I-765](https://www.uscis.gov/i-765) depends on the underlying petition. If revocation looks likely, brief the beneficiary on travel risk and EAD-renewal timing before the decision lands.
