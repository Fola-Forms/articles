Gov. Hochul has announced that New York State will allocate $7.25 million in funding for legal services for unaccompanied immigrant children. The funding comes after the Trump administration let a contract with a network of legal groups expire in July. This emergency infusion targets a critical gap in representation capacity affecting your caseload and client access to counsel.

## What changed

Governor Hochul, alongside Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Attorney General Letitia James, announced $7.25 million in state funding to support legal services for an estimated 1,400 unaccompanied minors in New York, about 250 of whom are currently detained.

The state allocated $7.25 million to a consortium of legal providers led by the Hispanic Federation to provide legal services to unaccompanied children fighting deportation. The funding is meant to fill a hole left by the federal government that allowed a multimillion-dollar contract to lapse.

The trigger for this state action was the federal cutoff. The Trump administration let a contract with a network of legal groups expire in July, and the $356 million annual contract with around 100 legal aid providers expired in July, while the government awarded a new $150 million contract to a Texas law firm that only covers children still in federal custody.

## Why it matters

This state commitment directly addresses representation gaps your clients face. Children who entered the country without a parent have been afforded special protections for the last two decades, including the roughly 1,400 kids in New York state. Without legal counsel, these children face removal proceedings alone.

The federal funding lapse left dozens of experienced nonprofit providers unable to continue their work. About 1,400 unaccompanied immigrant children in New York, including roughly 700 upstate, were at risk of losing their court representation after the Trump administration terminated its contract with the Acacia Center for Justice; the children are represented by nine New York organizations within Acacia's network, including the Empire Justice Center.

The new state allocation does not fully replace lost federal funding—the prior contract was worth far more—but it keeps critical providers operational and children in representation through a consortium led by the Hispanic Federation. This is the immediate lifeline preserving access to counsel in your removal and special immigrant juvenile status (SIJUS) cases.

## Way forward

- **Verify your provider network's participation.** Check whether the organizations you refer clients to or work with are included in the Hispanic Federation-led consortium receiving state funding. Confirm their status and capacity.

- **Update your client resources list.** If federal funding loss caused a provider to close or restrict intake, confirm new arrangements and update your referral protocols for unaccompanied minors.

- **Track the contract terms.** The $7.25 million is an emergency allocation. Confirm the contract end date and whether there is a path to renewal or expansion beyond current funding levels.

- **Distinguish from the new federal contract.** The new $150 million federal contract to Burke Law Group covers only children "in federal custody." Coordinate with providers to understand which clients fall under state vs. federal representation, and whether sponsorship or release from federal custody affects coverage.
